## 1. Owner decision

The project owner explicitly approved **Option C — No Destructive Authority** as the system's default authorization model.

Until independently controlled destructive-operation coverage is demonstrated and separately approved, the system must not grant destructive authority to any worker, adapter, service, operator or individual. This is a default deny boundary, not a temporary timeout or an implied permission to proceed after a waiting period.

Option C is selected as the current default. Option A is a possible future transition target, not selected now. Option B is not selected and remains unavailable for selection while P1.8.120 finding IR120-01 is unresolved.

## 2. Effective policy boundary

The following are permitted only within existing read-only access and applicable data-protection constraints:
- read and inspect lifecycle metadata;
- enumerate and reconcile manifests and inventory;
- perform non-mutating integrity checks;
- assess dependencies, holds, consumer declarations and lifecycle eligibility;
- prepare a deletion or archive assessment/request for review, without executing it.

The following remain prohibited and unauthorized:
- delete, overwrite, truncate, purge or otherwise destructively mutate canonical or copied data;
- archive or transition data into a destructive/irreversible lifecycle state;
- vacuum or run cleanup operations that can remove protected records or evidence;
- execute storage mutations through workers, adapters, queues, delegated credentials or alternate identities;
- grant or activate IAM roles, credentials, workflow permissions or storage capabilities that enable the above actions.

A lifecycle assessment, checkpoint closure, consumer ACK, restore readiness, quarantine resolution, trusted timestamp, evidence PASS or owner request does not itself grant destructive authority.

## 3. Mandatory fail-closed behavior

Any missing, stale, conflicting, unverifiable or incomplete dependency, consumer, hold, copy, restore, registry, trusted-time, authorization or mutation-fence evidence must remain protective and block destructive progression.

No timeout, heartbeat loss, disconnected consumer, retry, queue redelivery, restore, replay, role substitution or alternate execution path may implicitly release a hold or create authority.

No self-approval or incompatible authority combination is permitted. No destructive capability may be inferred from repository write access or from approval of this documentary decision.

## 4. Conditions for a future C-to-A proposal

A future proposal to move from C to A requires, at minimum:
1. At least two genuinely independent principals/actors, not merely multiple accounts controlled by one person.
2. The requester cannot approve their own request.
3. The approver is independent of the storage executor and can reject/block the exact operation before mutation.
4. The executor can act only on an immutable, narrowly scoped authorization bound to exact object, version/generation, operation and protected decision vector.
5. An independent verifier checks the post-operation result and evidence.
6. Revocation, emergency access, delegation, alternate credentials and effective-permission paths are inventoried and tested for bypass resistance.
7. The complete design receives independent review and a separate explicit owner approval before any IAM, implementation or operational change.

Until every prerequisite is evidenced and approved, Option C remains in force. This record does not pre-approve the transition.

## 5. Relationship to P1.8.120

P1.8.120 found IR120-01 MAJOR: Option B lacks a mandatory, technically enforced independent pre-commit veto/control. This owner decision avoids that unresolved model rather than weakening the requirement. IR120-01 remains relevant to Option B and is not represented as closed by selecting C.

The owner selection resolves the role-model direction at the documentary decision level. It does not establish implementation completeness, effective permissions, runtime enforcement or operational readiness.
