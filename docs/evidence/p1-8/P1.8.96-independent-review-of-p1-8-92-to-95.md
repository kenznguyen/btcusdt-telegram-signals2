## 4. Findings

### IR-01 — MAJOR: Canonical transition crosswalk still lacks complete authority for blocked-state recovery and reconciliation resume

**Evidence:** P1.8.92 §§2, 4, 6.

P1.8.92 improves the state vocabulary and maps the principal legacy labels. However:
- BLOCKED may return to ELIGIBILITY_REVIEW or FINAL_RECHECK after a separately recorded resolution, but the resolving authority, required event, prior-state guard and required revalidation scope are not defined.
- RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED may return to FINAL_RECHECK through a governed resume decision, but the decision authority, per-object reconciliation evidence threshold and exact event are not specified.
- The transition table permits “Any trusted observer/orchestrator” to enter RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED without defining the trusted observer class or event authorization.
- EXPIRED and REVOKED are defined as states, but their full transition/authority/event rows are folded into a generic pre-mutation terminal row. The distinct trigger and ordering evidence should be explicit.

This leaves implementers room to create incompatible recovery paths or grant a broad observer the ability to mutate request state.

**Required resolution:** add explicit transition rows for BLOCKED resolution, reconciliation resume, expiry and revocation, including actor/authority, event, prior revision, evidence prerequisites, per-object state checks and whether a new approval/authorization is required. Define observer authority as report-only unless separately delegated.

### IR-02 — MAJOR: P1.8.94 overstates coverage for backup-chain ambiguity and ledger obligations

**Evidence:** P1.8.94 N19 and N24 mappings; P1.8.90 NEG-ADP-07 and NEG-T3-01.

- N19 is “Backup chain member missing or ambiguous” but maps to NEG-ADP-07, whose scenario is removal of a backup-chain member that invalidates another recovery point. These are related recovery concerns but not the same negative condition. A missing/ambiguous chain member needs its own vector.
- N24 is “CORE_LEDGER or AI_LEDGER lineage/obligation unresolved” but maps to NEG-T3-01, whose scenario is conflating the two ledgers in one scope. Separation is not equivalent to unresolved lineage or obligation.

The traceability matrix therefore claims coverage that the mapped vectors do not directly prove.

**Required resolution:** add dedicated vectors for (a) missing/ambiguous recovery-chain member and (b) unresolved CORE_LEDGER/AI_LEDGER lineage or obligation; update N19/N24 mappings and keep the original cases NOT_RUN.

### IR-03 — MAJOR: N09 evidence absence and provenance uncertainty are not directly tested

**Evidence:** P1.8.94 N09 mapping; P1.8.90 NEG-TIME-03 and NEG-EVD-02.

N09 covers evidence that is missing, stale, corrupted or of unknown provenance. NEG-TIME-03 addresses the absence of an approved numeric freshness policy; NEG-EVD-02 addresses tampering/provenance mismatch. Neither directly specifies an evidence item that is absent or cannot be resolved to its claimed source/scope.

**Required resolution:** add a vector that removes or withholds a required evidence reference and asserts BLOCKED/NOT_VERIFIED, and a vector for an evidence reference whose source/scope cannot be resolved. Do not treat a missing reference as proof of absence of an obligation.

### IR-04 — MINOR: Residual reference/index coverage is broader in the source case than the listed vectors

**Evidence:** P1.8.94 N36; P1.8.90 NEG-ADP-03–06.

The existing vectors cover object versions, SQLite/PostgreSQL residual representations and delayed provider effects. N36 also names unexplained references and indexes, which are not independently isolated as a test scenario.

**Recommended resolution:** add a bounded residual-reference/index vector or narrow the N36 coverage wording so it does not imply direct test coverage of unmodeled residual types.

### IR-05 — OBSERVATION: P1.8.93 is conservatively specified but may disqualify all current adapters

P1.8.93 requires the storage side effect and all relevant policy/dependency/hold/authorization revisions to share one linearization point or a transactionally coupled decision boundary. It correctly rejects read-then-write, polling, heartbeat/cache invalidation and lease-only substitutes.

This is a stringent requirement. No evidence currently demonstrates that any target backend can satisfy it. The correct current status is therefore NO ADAPTER QUALIFIED / NOT IMPLEMENTED, not a reason to weaken the protocol.

### IR-06 — OBSERVATION: P1.8.95 resolves contract placement, not runtime availability

The updated Dataset Consumer Contract §20 now contains a normative declaration model, qualitative classes, owner attribution, immutable revisions, UNKNOWN/CONFLICTED behavior and archive gates. It preserves ACK/release separation and keeps numeric values OPEN.

No actual T0–T4 consumer declaration, authoritative registry or archive/restore path was inspected. SR-05 is resolved at documentary alignment level only.

### IR-07 — OBSERVATION: No destructive authority escalation found

The reviewed changes do not grant deletion authority, archive authority, self-approval, automatic release, or runtime mutation. T0 canonical data remains protected; CORE_LEDGER and AI_LEDGER remain separate. This is a documentary consistency observation, not runtime proof.
