# P1.8.85 — Hold Lifecycle and Mutation-Window Fencing Contract

Date: 2026-11-09  
Status: DOCUMENTARY DESIGN ONLY — NOT IMPLEMENTED / NOT ACTIVE / NOT AUTHORIZED  
Closes review findings: DR-01 (MAJOR), DR-03 (MAJOR)

## 1. Purpose and authority

This contract defines how protective holds are created, scoped, propagated, changed and enforced against archive or deletion decisions, including races during multi-object execution.

It supplements, and does not weaken, the Data Deletion Safety Contract, Central Data Lifecycle Policy, Dataset Consumer Contract, Copy/Backup/Replica Lifecycle Contract, P1.8.82 and P1.8.84. If requirements conflict, the more protective rule applies and the conflict blocks disposition pending an explicit governed correction. This document grants no archive, deletion, cleanup, key-destruction or storage-mutation authority.

A hold is a protective constraint, not a retention timer, deletion request, dependency release or execution authorization.

## 2. Hold identity and scope

Every hold record must bind:
- immutable hold ID and monotonically ordered revision;
- issuing authority, authenticated actor, role and delegation proof;
- reason category and non-sensitive rationale reference;
- exact subject identity: dataset/version/object/copy IDs and immutable manifest or lineage references;
- scope semantics, including whether the hold applies to source, known descendants, copies, derived materializations, evidence and recovery chains;
- effective event identity/time evidence, creation event and audit linkage;
- status, supersession/revocation reference and any review metadata.

Wildcards, mutable aliases, path-only identity, unbounded predicates and inferred scope are not sufficient. If lineage or scope cannot be resolved, the affected candidate set remains protected. A hold must not be narrowed by a downstream consumer, executor or storage adapter.

## 3. Hold lifecycle

The normative logical states are:

- DRAFT — not yet authoritative; cannot be used to claim a hold exists or is absent.
- ACTIVE — authoritative protective constraint; blocks incompatible disposition.
- REVIEW_DUE — still ACTIVE; review due date is an escalation signal only.
- CHANGE_PENDING — proposed scope or authority change; existing ACTIVE protection remains.
- REVOCATION_PENDING — revocation requested; existing ACTIVE protection remains.
- REVOKED — authoritative revocation event committed by authorized authority; applies only to its exact scope and does not retroactively validate earlier decisions.
- SUPERSEDED — replaced by a linked newer record; protection is continuous unless the authoritative transition explicitly proves a non-overlapping scope.
- CONFLICTED — inconsistent authority, revision, identity, scope or event ordering; fail-closed.
- UNKNOWN — state cannot be established; fail-closed.

Only an authorized hold authority may create, change or revoke a hold. A reviewer may identify a conflict but cannot silently rewrite the hold. A hold's review date or nominal expiry never causes automatic revocation. Revocation requires an explicit authenticated event, scope, authority and durable audit record.

## 4. Propagation and inheritance

A source hold is protective for every known copy, replica, backup, export, derived object or recovery-chain component that contains, represents or depends on the held source, unless an exact-scope adjudication demonstrates that the object is outside the hold. Copy-specific holds remain independent.

Propagation is not complete merely because an event was published or a registry row was updated. The system must record:
- source hold revision and event identity;
- discovered affected copy/dependency identities;
- each relevant authoritative registry/adapter boundary;
- acknowledgement or independently observable enforcement state;
- unresolved, unreachable or unmanaged destinations.

Unknown lineage, incomplete inventory, unavailable registry, missing acknowledgement or conflicting revision keeps the affected scope protective. Acknowledgement is evidence of receipt only; it is not proof of enforcement or a disposition grant.

## 5. Authoritative ordering and mutation-window fencing

A hold creation or scope expansion becomes protective at its durable commit in the authoritative hold registry. A revocation becomes effective only at its durable authorized commit. The registry must expose a monotonically ordered revision or equivalent non-replayable decision token.

An authorization package binds the exact hold-registry revision and relevant scope snapshot. Before every object mutation, the executor/adapter must enforce an atomic compare-and-act precondition against the current authoritative revision and exact object identity. A read-then-write check without a backend-enforced precondition is insufficient.

For a multi-object request:
1. Each object is an independently guarded mutation unit.
2. Before mutating object N, the adapter verifies that the request remains authorized, the manifest is unchanged, the relevant hold/dependency/policy revisions remain acceptable, and the object generation/identity still matches.
3. A new hold, scope expansion, revocation, revision conflict, registry partition or inability to prove ordering blocks the next mutation.
4. Already completed mutations are not presumed reversible. The request enters RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED; remaining objects are not processed.
5. The system records the exact last confirmed object, last authoritative revision, pending/ambiguous objects and residual copy obligations.

If a storage backend cannot atomically enforce the precondition at the mutation boundary, it is not eligible for destructive multi-object execution. A periodic poll, heartbeat, cache invalidation or best-effort notification is not an equivalent fence.

## 6. Cancellation, revocation and partition behavior

Cancellation, authorization revocation, hold creation/expansion and object mutation must have a defined authoritative ordering. If ordering cannot be established, no new mutation may begin and the outcome is RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED.

Registry unavailable, split-brain, stale leader, stale revision, duplicate/reordered event, signature/identity failure, clock uncertainty or audit discontinuity is fail-closed. No stale replica or read model may authorize absence of a hold.

A hold revocation after a completed mutation does not erase the historical decision or automatically restart a request. Re-evaluation requires a new decision package and authorization.

## 7. Evidence and verification

Evidence must support hold identity, actor authority, exact scope, ordered revisions, propagation coverage, adapter preconditions, per-object decision and mutation results, and independent reconciliation. Evidence must be tamper-evident and must not contain deleted payload, credentials, private keys or unnecessary sensitive data.

The verifier must not rely solely on executor logs or the same mutable read model used by the executor. If independent hold-state or mutation evidence is unavailable, the result is NOT VERIFIED.

## 8. Required negative cases

At minimum, a future isolated test specification must cover:
- hold created after approval but before execution authorization;
- hold created between two object mutations;
- source hold propagation to backup, replica, export and derived object;
- copy-specific hold with no source hold;
- revocation requested but not committed;
- review date elapsed without revocation;
- duplicate, reordered and replayed hold events;
- stale registry leader/revision and split-brain;
- registry outage during preflight and between object mutations;
- object generation/path substitution after preflight;
- propagation acknowledgement without enforcement proof;
- unknown external destination or incomplete lineage.

These are design requirements, not tests executed or approved.
