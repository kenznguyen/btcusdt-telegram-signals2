## 3. Overall assessment

The design consistently preserves these safety principles:
- eligibility, approval, execution authorization, mutation and independent verification remain distinct;
- unknown or conflicting evidence fails closed;
- ACK, timeout, age, TTL and missing heartbeat do not release dependencies or authorize disposition;
- source and copy lifecycles are separate; no implicit cascade;
- executor success is not independent proof;
- ambiguous/partial outcomes require reconciliation before retry;
- T0 canonical data and T3 CORE_LEDGER/AI_LEDGER remain protected and distinct;
- no retention duration, grace, freshness window, quorum, RPO/RTO, provider or deletion exception is invented.

No reviewed document grants destructive authority. No unsafe direct contradiction was found that would authorize a prohibited operation.

However, important contracts are still design-only and several foundational decisions remain OPEN. The system is not implementation-ready or operationally accepted.

## 4. Findings

### SR-01 — MAJOR: Three request-state vocabularies are not mapped into one normative transition contract

**Evidence:** Data Deletion Safety Contract §4; P1.8.82 §5; P1.8.86 §§2.5–2.8.

The governing Deletion Safety Contract uses states such as `Proposed`, `Eligibility review`, `Eligible for approval`, `Approved`, `Awaiting execution authorization`, `Execution authorized`, `Executing`, `Reconciliation required`, and `Verified complete`.

P1.8.82 introduces a separate sequence `REQUESTED → REVIEWED_ELIGIBLE → APPROVED → FINAL_RECHECK → AUTHORIZED → EXECUTING → RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED → VERIFIED_COMPLETE`. P1.8.86 introduces another request-domain vocabulary including `PROPOSED`, `ELIGIBILITY_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE_FOR_APPROVAL`, `AWAITING_EXECUTION_AUTHORIZATION`, and `VERIFIED_COMPLETE_WITHIN_SCOPE`.

The intent is compatible, but the documents do not define a canonical crosswalk, authoritative owner for each transition, or explicit mapping for rejection, expiry, cancellation, blocking and re-entry. Three parallel vocabularies can produce divergent implementations or ambiguous audit interpretation.

**Required resolution:** publish one normative transition table mapping legacy/generic labels to canonical domain states, guards, actor authority, event, terminality and re-entry. Preserve separate state domains; do not collapse them into one global enum.

### SR-02 — MAJOR: Mutation-window fencing is specified as a requirement but its cross-system atomicity mechanism remains unresolved

**Evidence:** P1.8.85 §5; P1.8.86 §6 STATE-05; P1.8.81 §§4.2–4.3; P1.8.82 §§5–9.

P1.8.85 requires an atomic compare-and-act precondition against the authoritative hold revision at each storage mutation. P1.8.86 leaves the cross-domain transaction/fencing mechanism OPEN. The dependency registry and storage adapter are separate logical boundaries, and no concrete protocol proves that a hold/dependency/policy revision cannot change after the final check but before the backend mutation.

The contract correctly rejects a read-then-write check and says an adapter lacking backend-enforced preconditions is ineligible. However, no adapter capability or cross-system linearization point is demonstrated.

**Required resolution:** before implementation, define a backend-enforceable fencing protocol or transaction boundary, including token issuance, monotonic revision, object generation precondition, revocation/hold race ordering, registry outage behavior and proof that stale tokens cannot mutate. If a backend cannot satisfy it, keep that backend permanently ineligible for destructive operations.

### SR-03 — MAJOR: Negative vectors lack explicit traceability to the original 45-case matrix and several race dimensions

**Evidence:** P1.8.84 §4; P1.8.90 §§2–5.

P1.8.90 provides 47 useful deterministic vectors, fixture requirements and independent-oracle rules. It says the catalogue supplements P1.8.84, but does not provide a one-to-one traceability map showing which original N01–N45 cases are covered, expanded, superseded or still uncovered.

Some concurrency cases are only described at a high level. The catalogue does not explicitly isolate every critical interleaving, including hold creation/revocation after mutation submission but before backend confirmation, authorization revocation during an in-flight object operation, policy revision during a multi-object batch, and an audit/registry outage immediately after a backend side effect.

**Required resolution:** add a traceability matrix for N01–N45 to NEG-* IDs and identify uncovered cases. Define deterministic event schedules and exact per-object assertions for the missing interleavings. Keep all execution separately gated and synthetic-only.

### SR-04 — MAJOR: Personal-project separation of duties has no approved operational approval path

**Evidence:** P1.8.82 §2 and AUTH-01; P1.8.88 §3 and ID-05/ID-06; P1.8.84 ACC-03.

The documents correctly state that the same principal cannot be requester and independent approver, and that a service account cannot be presented as an independent human. They also state no compensating control is approved. For a single-owner project, this means destructive approval cannot currently be completed.

This is a deliberate safe block, not a defect to solve by weakening separation of duties.

**Required resolution:** remain BLOCKED until the owner explicitly selects and approves a genuinely independent approver model or separately reviews a defensible compensating-control proposal. No self-approval exception is inferred.

### SR-05 — MAJOR: Archive availability declaration is not yet fully reconciled into the governing consumer contract

**Evidence:** Dataset Consumer Contract §§5–6, 17; P1.8.81 §§3 and 9; P1.8.80 RET-04; prior cross-contract review COPY-04.

P1.8.81 defines consumer availability classes and declaration fields, but the governing Dataset Consumer Contract still leaves operational availability targets and registry details OPEN and does not itself carry the complete normative declaration schema. The cross-contract review already identifies archive availability as an OPEN gap.

The safe outcome is explicit: archive remains BLOCKED when requirements are unknown. This gap does not authorize deletion or justify guessing latency/SLO values.

**Required resolution:** either formally incorporate P1.8.81 as a normative extension to the Dataset Consumer Contract with versioned precedence, or update the governing contract through an approved documentation change. Keep archive blocked until consumer-specific evidence exists.

### SR-06 — MAJOR: Trusted time, identity issuer and evidence freshness remain unbound

**Evidence:** P1.8.88 §§4–8; P1.8.85 §§5–7; P1.8.82 AUTH-02/AUTH-05/AUTH-06/AUTH-07.

The failure behavior is appropriately fail-closed, but there is no selected identity issuer, authentication assurance, trusted time source, monotonic ordering service, skew bound, freshness window or authorization lifetime. These are not harmless implementation details: they determine whether expiry, revocation, approval and event ordering can be trusted.

**Required resolution:** keep all time-dependent authorization BLOCKED until sources and bounds are measured, risk-reviewed and explicitly approved. Do not substitute local wall clocks or invent defaults.

### SR-07 — MAJOR: Inventory completeness and external-copy coverage are still NOT VERIFIED

**Evidence:** P1.8.83 §§3–4/9; P1.8.89 §§2–6/8; Static Inventory Baseline §§5 and 8; Environment Boundary §§3–5.

The design correctly limits claims to declared discovery boundaries. Runtime copies, local devices, external recipients, provider-side backups, replicas and complete consumer dependencies remain unknown. The experimental VPS inventory has not been performed in this review; official environment remains NOT DESIGNATED.

**Required resolution:** no global “all copies known/deleted” claim. Any future inventory needs separate bounded authorization, discovery authority, exclusions, coverage proof and experimental-vs-official evidence labels.

### SR-08 — MAJOR: Adapter and independent-verifier capabilities remain documentary only

**Evidence:** P1.8.83 §6; P1.8.87 §§3–6/7; P1.8.82 §§6/9/12; P1.8.84 ACC-12/ACC-13.

SQLite/filesystem, PostgreSQL, object storage, backup chain, exports and derived artifacts have useful semantic profiles, but there is no implemented adapter, provider-specific guarantee, independent oracle or negative-test evidence. Generic semantics cannot prove that a particular backend can honor exact scope, fencing, idempotency, residual search and independent verification.

**Required resolution:** no adapter approval or real-storage test. Each adapter needs a separately reviewed capability contract and isolated synthetic evidence before any future consideration.

### SR-09 — MAJOR: Retention and deletion policy remains owner-incomplete

**Evidence:** P1.8.80 §§4/9; P1.8.84 §10; Central Data Lifecycle Policy §§7–14.

No data class has been granted deletion permission. T0 absolute non-deletability vs narrow exception remains OPEN. Numeric retention floors, evidence/tombstone retention, emergency/legal authority and class-specific end conditions remain OPEN.

This is consistent with the user's demand-based retention direction and is safer than inventing TTLs. It means the lifecycle policy is not operationally complete and no deletion candidate can be presumed eligible.

**Required resolution:** owner decisions must be explicit and evidence-led, class by class. Preserve current protected defaults until then.

### SR-10 — MINOR: “Executable” in P1.8.90 title may be mistaken for implemented tests

**Evidence:** P1.8.90 title and §§1, 9.

The body correctly says this is test design only and no code/tests were created or run. The title could nevertheless be read as an assertion of executable test code.

**Recommended resolution:** consider renaming to “Deterministic Negative-Test Specification” while preserving the explicit NOT EXECUTED status. This is editorial, not a runtime blocker.

### SR-11 — OBSERVATION: Copy-contract section numbering correction verified

The corrected contract now orders §5.5 Hold inheritance before §5.6 Evidence copies. Content/authority remain unchanged. Read-back confirmed the corrected blob. No additional finding remains for the prior DR-09.

### SR-12 — OBSERVATION: No unsafe cross-contract authority escalation found

Across the reviewed set, consumer ACK does not release dependency, source deletion does not cascade to copies, archive is distinct from deletion, approval does not equal execution authorization, and executor success does not equal independent verification. T0 and CORE_LEDGER/AI_LEDGER protection remains intact.

This observation is limited to documentary consistency; it is not runtime proof.
