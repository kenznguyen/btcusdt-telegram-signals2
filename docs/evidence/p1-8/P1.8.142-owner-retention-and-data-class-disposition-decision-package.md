## 2. Non-negotiable constraints

1. **No delete-after-read.** A successful read, consumer ACK, job completion, model training completion, or elapsed time does not itself end retention.
2. **No automatic deletion.** No TTL, age rule, low-access rule, storage-pressure rule, heartbeat timeout, or scheduled job may authorize disposition.
3. **No cascade.** Source, replica, backup, export, snapshot, cache, derived output, model, ledger and evidence copy retain separate lifecycle identities.
4. **T0 remains an independent canonical asset.** T0 canonical source data is protected by default; no consumer, strategy or storage operator receives unilateral authority over it.
5. **Ledgers remain separate and protected.** CORE_LEDGER and AI_LEDGER cannot be merged, netted, or deleted for lifecycle convenience.
6. **Unknown means protected.** Unknown owner, class, lineage, dependencies, copies, holds, legal/audit duty, restore state or evidence freshness blocks disposition.
7. **Archive is not a loophole.** Under the current Option C decision, archive and other irreversible/destructive transitions are not executable. A future archive policy decision would not grant execution authority.
8. **Option C remains controlling.** No destructive authority is granted by a retention-policy decision. Any future change to that boundary requires a separate explicit owner decision.
9. **No numeric values by assumption.** Retention floors, TTLs, grace periods, review intervals, authorization lifetime and evidence freshness remain unset until evidence and owner approval support them.
10. **No legal interpretation by the system.** Legal/privacy obligations require competent review and exact-scope evidence.

## 3. Proposed policy vocabulary

| Policy class | Meaning | Effect under current Option C |
|---|---|---|
| PROTECTED | Data has canonical, operational, audit, ledger, recovery, security or other continuing obligations | Preserve; no disposition execution |
| RETAIN_UNTIL_OBLIGATIONS_CLOSE | Preserve until every named obligation has an evidenced end condition | Review/escalation only |
| REVIEWABLE_NONCANONICAL | A specific immutable object/version may enter read-only eligibility review after all gates are evidenced | Review only; no authorization or execution |
| COPY_SPECIFIC_REVIEW | A separately identified copy may be assessed independently of its source | Review only; no cascade or execution |
| BLOCKED_UNKNOWN | Inventory, ownership, lineage, dependencies, holds or policy are incomplete | Fail closed; preserve |
| OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED | Policy class or exception has not been ratified | Preserve; do not infer a default |

A reviewable class is not a disposable class. A review result cannot trigger an operation.

### 4.1 T0 policy recommendation

Recommended owner choice: **T0 canonical data is non-deletable by default, with no routine lifecycle deletion path.**

If the owner wishes to preserve a theoretical exception for a binding legal/security requirement, it must be handled as a separate, exact-scope policy amendment with competent review, independent evidence and a new owner decision. Such an exception must not become a general cleanup mechanism, and it does not grant current execution authority.

Alternative choices:
- **A — Absolute non-deletability:** no deletion exception under any system policy; external legal conflict requires separate governance outside the normal lifecycle.
- **B — Protected by default with exceptional review:** no routine deletion; narrowly scoped exception may be reviewed for binding legal/security requirements, but remains blocked under current Option C until separately authorized.
- **C — General eligibility framework:** T0 may enter ordinary object-level deletion review after dependencies close. **Not recommended** because it weakens the independent canonical data-asset boundary.

Recommendation: **B**, with the exception strictly outside normal lifecycle automation and no current destructive authority.

### 4.2 Which classes may ever be considered for deletion review?

Recommended limited reviewable set:
- T4 deterministic caches/read models/materializations;
- T2 ephemeral inference outputs proven not to underpin material decisions or claims;
- T1 raw observations only when exact dependent outputs remain reproducible from retained, authoritative inputs and all other obligations are closed;
- individually identified noncanonical copies after copy-specific recovery/hold/dependency checks.

Explicitly excluded from class-wide deletion review:
- T0 canonical source data;
- CORE_LEDGER and AI_LEDGER;
- T3 event/reconciliation/audit records while needed to reconstruct paper outcomes;
- governance approvals, policy history, tombstones and evidence needed to prove lifecycle actions;
- any unknown or incomplete inventory.

Even for the limited reviewable set, the current permitted effect is read-only review only.

## 5. Retention end-condition model

For each exact dataset version or copy, retention remains active until every applicable obligation has a positive, attributable end condition:

1. canonical/producer obligation;
2. active consumer dependencies;
3. replay, correction, backtest, training, evaluation and inference reproducibility;
4. material signal/risk/ledger decision reconstruction;
5. incident, security, legal, privacy, contractual and audit hold;
6. recovery and backup-chain obligations;
7. source/copy lineage and external recipient obligations;
8. evidence and historical verification obligations.

The effective retention end is the latest unresolved end condition. If any obligation has no approved end condition, the result remains RETAIN_UNTIL_OBLIGATIONS_CLOSE or BLOCKED_UNKNOWN.

A review date creates an owner task only. It cannot release dependencies, expire a hold or trigger a lifecycle mutation.

## 6. Archive policy recommendation

Under the currently ratified Option C, archive is **not executable**. This package recommends:
- preserve archive as a future non-destructive design concept only;
- do not treat archive as deletion eligibility or as a way to evade source/copy governance;
- require consumer availability, access latency, restore-time, integrity, encryption, lineage and recovery-chain evidence before any future archive policy is considered;
- require a separate owner decision before any archive execution authority is introduced.

No archive provider, storage tier, numeric restore target or schedule is selected.

## 7. Copy and evidence retention

1. Every copy has its own identity, source link, custodian, class, completeness, location and hold/dependency set.
2. Source holds propagate protectively to all known relevant copies; unresolved lineage keeps copies protected.
3. Backup retention is evaluated by complete recoverable chain, not isolated file age.
4. Replica disposition must preserve availability and verified failover/rebuild obligations.
5. Exports/local copies require recipient and destination accounting; transfer completion is not proof of copy removal.
6. Evidence copies remain protected while needed to support an acceptance, security, incident, financial reconstruction or lifecycle claim.
7. GitHub source history and CI artifacts do not substitute for operational audit logs or verified backups.
8. No source-to-copy or copy-to-child cascade is permitted.

## 9. Consequences of the recommended policy

Benefits:
- protects T0 as an independent canonical asset;
- prevents consumer completion from being mistaken for data disposability;
- preserves ledger and model decision reconstruction;
- limits future review to noncanonical, demonstrably reproducible or copy-specific objects;
- avoids invented retention periods and unverified storage-provider assumptions;
- remains compatible with Option C and current paper-only posture.

Costs and trade-offs:
- storage usage may grow until class-specific obligations and copy inventories are measured;
- some stale caches/exports may remain longer than operationally necessary;
- archive and deletion automation remain unavailable;
- exact retention floors cannot be advertised until the owner resolves policy and evidence requirements.

The storage cost is accepted as the safer temporary trade-off. Storage pressure is not an exception to the policy.

## 10. Explicit non-authorization

This package does not:
- approve a deletion class or a particular object for deletion;
- grant archive, cleanup, vacuum, compaction, backup-expiry or replica-removal authority;
- select numeric retention durations, TTLs, grace periods or freshness windows;
- create or modify schemas, APIs, registries, schedulers, services, adapters, credentials or permissions;

- resume the paused GitHub permission inventory;
- change T0/T1/T2/T3/T4 runtime, VPS, database, Feeder, strategies, ledgers or risk controls;
- authorize live trading or real orders.

All operational and destructive actions remain separately gated. Option C remains in force.
