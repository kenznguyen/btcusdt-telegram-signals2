## 1. Purpose and scope

This assessment consolidates the data lifecycle design from the foundational architecture contracts and P1.8.80–P1.8.140. It checks:

- retention and eligibility policy;
- dataset, consumer dependency, hold, copy, evidence and deletion-request state separation;
- canonical request transitions and per-object reconciliation;
- authorization separation and the owner-approved Option C boundary;
- registry-to-storage mutation fencing;
- negative-case specification and independent-oracle requirements;
- consumer availability and archive compatibility;
- V07 trusted-time/revocation, V08 checkpoint/seal, Restore and TSA evidence integration;
- remaining owner decisions, implementation prerequisites and evidence gaps.

The review is limited to repository documents on the existing branch. It does not inspect live runtime state, actual data, databases, backups, replicas, providers, credentials, GitHub effective permissions or external recipients.

The GitHub permission inventory is explicitly **PAUSED by owner instruction**. This assessment does not resume it, propose a collector identity, call additional inventory endpoints or expand its scope.

## 3. Executive conclusion

**The core safety architecture is coherent at documentary level. It is not complete enough to declare the lifecycle design closed, implementation-ready or operationally accepted.**

No direct cross-contract contradiction was found that grants a destructive path contrary to Option C. The most important invariants are consistently represented:

1. Retention follows declared demand and obligations, not delete-after-read, age, size, storage pressure or TTL.
2. Consumer ACK, dependency release, eligibility, approval, execution authorization, storage mutation and independent verification are separate events/authorities.
3. T0 remains an independent canonical data asset; consumers do not gain authority over T0 source data.
4. CORE_LEDGER and AI_LEDGER remain separate and independently attributable.
5. Source, replica, backup, export, checkpoint, correction, model and evidence copies have separate lifecycle identities.
6. No implicit cascade, timeout-based release, blind retry or inference from missing logs/heartbeats is permitted.
7. Ambiguous or partially completed operations require per-object reconciliation; request-level terminality cannot hide an unknown object outcome.
8. Restore readiness, V07 resolution, V08 closure, TSA verification, consumer ACK or lifecycle eligibility cannot create deletion authority.
9. Option C is the current owner-approved default: no destructive authority is granted.
10. Missing, stale, conflicting, incomplete or unverifiable evidence remains protective/fail-closed.

## 5. Findings and unresolved gates

These findings describe residual design/assurance work, not evidence that a production system has failed.

### LDA-01 — MAJOR: Retention and class-specific disposition policy is not owner-complete

**Evidence:** P1.8.80 §§4, 7 and 9; P1.8.84 §10; Central Data Lifecycle Policy; P1.8.121.

The framework classifies T0–T4, ledgers, evidence and copies, but it intentionally leaves open:
- whether T0 canonical data is absolutely non-deletable or has a narrowly governed exception;
- which non-T0 classes may ever enter deletion eligibility review;
- class-specific minimum preservation floors and end conditions;
- tombstone/audit/evidence retention;
- legal/emergency authority and conflict handling.

Option C currently prevents destructive action regardless of these future policy choices. It does not silently resolve the long-term policy questions.

**Required disposition:** preserve all current protected defaults. Do not invent TTLs, retention durations, delete-after-use behavior or class-wide eligibility. Any later class policy must be an explicit owner decision with evidence and independent review.

### LDA-02 — MAJOR: V07 trusted-time/revocation is not parameter- or provider-ready

**Evidence:** P1.8.34, P1.8.36–P1.8.43, P1.8.107, P1.8.112, P1.8.117 and P1.8.40–P1.8.43 parameter inventory/review.

The design now binds the exact signature, verification context, trusted-time evidence, trust/revocation revisions and final mutation decision vector. However, trust authority/profile, trust-anchor lifecycle, TSA protocol, algorithm/encoding, timestamp uncertainty, revocation authority/effective-time semantics, freshness limits, propagation and equality boundaries remain OPEN. Cryptographic binding fixture design is not equivalent to historical eligibility or operational trust.

**Required disposition:** V07 remains BLOCKED. No TSA/provider/key/trust-anchor operation or numeric parameter is inferred.

### LDA-03 — MAJOR: V08 checkpoint closure is specified but actual durable ordering is unproven

**Evidence:** P1.8.34, P1.8.106, P1.8.111, P1.8.40, P1.8.44–P1.8.46.

The contract correctly requires UTC half-open windows, source-progress evidence, watermark gating, immutable checkpoints, append-only corrections and a total-order proof for commit versus seal. Equality/overlapping uncertainty without a comparable authoritative ordering token is UNKNOWN/HOLD.

The actual durable commit/seal boundary, ordering-token semantics, partition progress, watermark/lateness, clock precision/uncertainty and correction/replay implementation remain OPEN.

**Required disposition:** V08 remains OPEN/BLOCKED. Do not infer checkpoint membership from timestamps, arrival order or a scalar watermark without complete partition/order proof.

### LDA-04 — MAJOR: Restore acceptance and recovery guarantees are not measured or operationally qualified

**Evidence:** P1.8.26, P1.8.28, P1.8.30, P1.8.34, P1.8.105, P1.8.110 and P1.8.116.

Restore epoch, governance epoch, operation-history HWM, revision-vector binding and post-restore reconciliation are specified. However, the failure domain, provider/database semantics, HWM completeness, transaction durability, independent-oracle availability and rehearsal independence are not demonstrated. Previously recorded RPO/RTO values are planning targets only: acknowledged-transaction RPO 0 within a tested boundary, snapshot RPO ≤5 minutes, staging RTO ≤60 minutes, and three independent rehearsals. They are not approved SLOs or measured capabilities.

**Required disposition:** Restore remains OPEN/BLOCKED. No staging provisioning, restore rehearsal or fault injection without a separate explicit approval and a frozen measurement contract.

### LDA-05 — MAJOR: Mutation fencing and adapter qualification remain unproven

**Evidence:** P1.8.85, P1.8.93, P1.8.102, P1.8.112 and P1.8.117.

The design correctly rejects read-then-write, polling, heartbeat, cache invalidation or a lease alone as substitutes for an atomic or provably ordered mutation boundary. The exact protected decision vector and per-object reconciliation behavior are documented. No actual backend or adapter has demonstrated that the storage side effect is linearized with policy, dependency, hold, authorization and trusted-time revisions.

**Required disposition:** no adapter is qualified for destructive use. Keep every destructive adapter INELIGIBLE until a provider-specific capability dossier and separately authorized isolated evidence prove the required semantics. Do not weaken the abstract fence to accommodate an unproven backend.

### LDA-06 — MAJOR: Runtime inventory and completeness of consumers/copies remain NOT VERIFIED

**Evidence:** P1.8.80, P1.8.83, P1.8.89, P1.8.113, P1.8.116, P1.8.118 and Dataset Consumer Inventory Baseline.

Static source inventory and graph contracts do not prove the deployed set of consumers, dependencies, backups, replicas, exports, caches, derived artifacts, external recipients or provider-side copies. An empty registry query or absent heartbeat cannot prove absence. Official environment status remains distinct from experimental VPS observations.

**Required disposition:** no global “all copies known”, “no remaining dependency” or “deleted everywhere” claim. Any future runtime inventory needs its own bounded approval and completeness evidence. This assessment does not authorize or perform that inventory.

### LDA-07 — MAJOR: Negative-case specifications have not been executed

**Evidence:** P1.8.84, P1.8.90, P1.8.94, P1.8.98 and P1.8.103.

The latest reviewed specification records 87 unique vectors (47 baseline plus 40 supplemental). P1.8.103 confirms the count and closes the P1.8.99 transition-safety findings at documentary specification level. All vectors remain NOT_RUN. Synthetic runner, fixture isolation, deterministic concurrency harness, independent oracle, artifact provenance and cleanup containment remain open.

**Required disposition:** do not call these vectors tests that passed. Any future synthetic execution requires separate approval, proven isolation from canonical/runtime data and an independent oracle. No production or destructive testing is authorized here.

### LDA-08 — MODERATE / HOLD: Option C technical enforcement remains NOT VERIFIED

**Evidence:** P1.8.121–P1.8.128 and P1.8.137–P1.8.140.

Option C is an unambiguous owner decision and is internally consistent at documentary level. It prohibits destructive authority across workers, adapters, services, operators and alternate identities. However, effective GitHub, workflow, runner, credential, OIDC and external-provider permissions have not been proven to enforce that boundary. P1.8.137–P1.8.140 stopped fail-closed because the collector identity/effective read-only boundary could not be established.

**Required disposition:** preserve Option C and the current inventory pause. Do not continue EP-02–EP-08, create a collector identity, inspect additional endpoints, create credentials or change permissions. Any resumption requires a new explicit owner decision. No technical-enforcement PASS may be claimed meanwhile.

### LDA-09 — MODERATE: Some lifecycle policy decisions are deliberately more restrictive than future archive/deletion design paths

**Evidence:** Central Data Lifecycle Policy §§8–9; P1.8.80; P1.8.121.

The policy describes archive review as a potentially available non-deletion transition, while the currently approved Option C prohibits archive or other destructive/irreversible lifecycle transitions as well as deletion. These are compatible because Option C is the controlling current authority boundary and P1.8.80 grants reviewability only, not execution permission.

**Required disposition:** state explicitly in any future implementation plan that archive remains non-executable under the current Option C decision. Do not interpret the general archive policy as an exception to the owner's current prohibition.
