# P1.8.89 — Inventory Claim Boundaries and Discovery Evidence

Date: 2026-11-09  
Status: DOCUMENTARY DESIGN ONLY — NOT IMPLEMENTED / NOT ACTIVE / NOT AUTHORIZED  
Closes review finding: DR-06 (MAJOR)

## 1. Purpose

This contract defines what an inventory can and cannot prove about datasets, consumers, copies and destinations. It distinguishes a bounded, evidenced inventory from an unsupported claim that all copies are known.

This is a discovery and claim framework, not authorization to inspect runtime systems. The experimental VPS inventory remains deferred/not performed; official environment remains NOT DESIGNATED.

## 2. Inventory boundary identity

Every inventory claim must declare:
- inventory ID, version, purpose and owner;
- exact source boundary, account/project/host/namespace and object classes;
- discovery authority and permissions used;
- method/tool/version and observation interval;
- coverage denominator or explainable coverage model;
- known exclusions and inaccessible destinations;
- identity, lineage and copy-generation confidence;
- integrity/provenance evidence and redaction method;
- unresolved, stale, conflicted and unmanaged findings;
- reviewer and claim expiry/revalidation trigger, if separately approved.

A claim must not exceed the observability of its discovery method. “No result found” is not proof of absence outside the declared boundary.

## 3. Required discovery boundaries

A future approved inventory plan must consider, as applicable:
- T0 canonical database, WAL/journal, snapshots, replicas and backups;
- T1 raw files, snapshots, sidecars, exports and collector working directories;
- T2 downloaded candles, features, training datasets, models, predictions, caches and experiment outputs;
- T3 paper database, CORE_LEDGER, AI_LEDGER, reservations, orders, audit/event journals, reconciliation and backups;
- T4 dashboard/read models, browser/device caches and user exports;
- Git repository objects, branches, releases, LFS and CI artifacts;
- host/container volumes, temporary directories, filesystem snapshots and attached storage;
- object-storage versions, lifecycle remnants, cross-region replicas and provider backups;
- local devices, downloads, removable media and user-managed copies;
- third-party recipients, shared links, integrations and external export destinations;
- governance, approval, execution, verification and tombstone evidence.

This list is a discovery checklist, not a claim that these stores exist or have been inspected.

## 4. Inventory outcomes

- VERIFIED_COMPLETE_WITHIN_SCOPE — all declared in-scope discovery methods completed and reconciled; exclusions remain explicit.
- PARTIAL — some required sources or objects were inaccessible or not reconciled.
- UNKNOWN — boundary, authority, coverage or identity cannot be established.
- CONFLICTED — sources disagree or lineage/ownership conflicts.
- STALE — evidence is older than an approved freshness criterion or a change trigger occurred.
- NOT_APPLICABLE — evidence demonstrates the boundary/class does not apply.

Only VERIFIED_COMPLETE_WITHIN_SCOPE supports a completeness statement, and only for its declared boundary and observation point. It does not establish global completeness.

Registry presence is not inventory completeness. Registry absence, an empty directory listing, no recent heartbeat, or no consumer response does not establish absence of data or dependency.

## 5. Claim levels

Permitted bounded claims:
- “All objects discoverable through source boundary B using method M were reconciled at observation point T.”
- “The inventory covers the named repository and CI artifacts; personal downloads and external recipients are excluded.”
- “Known copies within listed destinations are accounted for; unmanaged destinations remain unknown.”

Prohibited claims:
- “All copies are known” without complete discovery authority over every relevant boundary.
- “No consumer exists” because registry or heartbeat is empty.
- “All copies deleted” while any relevant boundary is PARTIAL, UNKNOWN, CONFLICTED or STALE.
- “Global deletion complete” when external recipients or provider-side copies cannot be independently observed.

## 6. Inventory freshness and change triggers

Freshness must be based on an explicitly approved and measured policy; this contract sets no numeric window. Material triggers include new exports, deployments, branch/artifact publication, replication configuration changes, new consumers, storage migrations, access/ownership changes, backup creation, restore, or discovery-method failure.

A trigger invalidates the affected completeness claim until reconciliation. Missing heartbeat or elapsed time does not release dependencies or authorize disposition.

## 7. Evidence and privacy

Inventory evidence should record object identity, version, location class, lineage, owner, discovery method, coverage and uncertainty. It should avoid collecting payload content, secrets, access tokens or unrelated personal information. Sensitive locations may be represented by stable protected references with access controls.

A hash is useful only when the hashed object and canonicalization method are defined. A digest does not prove completeness or ownership.

## 9. Restrictions and acceptance

This contract does not authorize VPS access, database queries, filesystem scans, connector access, account enumeration, runtime inventory, data transfer or permission changes. Any such inventory requires a separate bounded scope and approval.

T0 canonical data remains protected and independently governed. T1–T4 classifications remain subject to their existing obligations; CORE_LEDGER and AI_LEDGER stay separate.

Design acceptance requires boundary declarations, discovery authority, coverage/exclusion model, outcome vocabulary and bounded claim rules. Runtime inventory remains NOT VERIFIED.
