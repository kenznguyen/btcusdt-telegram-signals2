## 3. Finding

### IR149-01 — MODERATE: Conflicted primary reviewer versus mandatory independent second reviewer is not defined consistently

P1.8.148 §2 states that the “primary independent reviewer MUST be independent” of every listed role. A person holding any such role is CONFLICTED.

P1.8.148 §3 then says that where full separation is infeasible, the assessment MUST use an independent second reviewer who examines the evidence and the “primary reviewer's conclusion.” Section 4 likewise requests a record of the “primary reviewer and independent second reviewer/control.” Section 6 permits a reviewer with a conflicting role to remain blocked only if no qualifying second review is completed.

These clauses leave two possible readings:
1. a conflicted role-holder may perform a preliminary/primary assessment, provided a fully independent second reviewer independently reviews the underlying evidence; or
2. the conflicted role-holder may not be a reviewer at all, and the independent second reviewer must become the sole qualifying reviewer, while the conflicted person is only an evidence producer/attester/requester.

The contract should select one model explicitly. Without this, “primary reviewer” could be read as granting review status to a conflicted person, despite §2's mandatory independence rule.

Required narrow closure:
- explicitly distinguish a conflicted contributor/evidence producer from a qualifying independent reviewer;
- state whether a conflicted person may prepare a non-authoritative preliminary assessment, but prohibit that person from issuing an independent review disposition or accepting the claim;
- identify the independent second reviewer as the qualifying reviewer for the affected claim, with direct evidence examination and sole authority to mark the review status;
- require the record to label any conflicted preliminary assessment as non-independent and non-accepting;
- retain fail-closed status if no qualifying independent reviewer is available.

This clarification must not reduce the mandatory second-review control or allow two conflicted persons to validate one another.
