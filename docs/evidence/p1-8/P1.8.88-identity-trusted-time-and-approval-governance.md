# P1.8.88 — Identity, Trusted Time and Approval Governance

Date: 2026-11-09  
Status: DOCUMENTARY DESIGN ONLY — NOT IMPLEMENTED / NOT ACTIVE / NOT AUTHORIZED  
Closes review findings: DR-05 (MAJOR), DR-08 (MAJOR)

## 1. Purpose and guardrail

This contract defines the identity, delegation, separation-of-duties and trusted-time requirements for lifecycle decisions. It does not select an identity provider, MFA vendor, quorum, clock service or numeric validity window. No destructive approval model is activated.

The project is personally owned. A practical workflow must not silently convert the same person's request and approval into independent approval. Until a valid role model and compensating controls are explicitly approved, destructive authorization remains unavailable.

## 2. Identity and authority model

Every human or service principal must have:
- stable unique subject ID, issuer and authentication context;
- authenticated session and assurance evidence appropriate to the action;
- explicitly granted role and exact scope;
- delegation chain, issuer, recipient, scope and validity where delegation exists;
- revocation status and authoritative revision;
- tamper-evident audit event.

Roles remain distinct: requester, dataset steward, consumer owner, lifecycle reviewer, independent approver, execution authority, storage executor, independent verifier and auditor. One identity may not impersonate another role by changing a client-supplied label.

Service identities are non-human principals with narrowly scoped capabilities, explicit owner, key rotation/revocation evidence and no interactive approval authority unless separately governed.

## 3. Separation of duties

Requester and independent approver must be different authenticated principals. Executor cannot approve, authorize or independently verify its own operation. Verifier must not rely exclusively on executor-controlled evidence.

Delegation does not erase the identity of the delegator or delegate. Delegation must be explicit, purpose-bound, scope-bound and auditable; transitive delegation is prohibited unless a future policy expressly allows and proves it.

For a single-owner personal project, there is currently no approved independent-approver arrangement. The project must not invent a second identity, treat a service account as a human approver, or claim independence from role-name separation alone.

A future compensating-control proposal may be considered only through separate owner approval and independent review. It must demonstrate why self-approval risk is controlled, how authorization is independently constrained, and how the verifier remains independent. No compensating control is approved by this document.

## 4. Authentication and session assurance

Authentication evidence must be checked at the decision boundary, not inferred from a previously authenticated session. Revoked, expired, downgraded or ambiguous identity assurance blocks approval/authorization. MFA requirements, credential assurance level and re-authentication interval remain OPEN pending explicit risk decision.

Secrets, tokens and private keys must never be written into manifests, tombstones or ordinary logs. Evidence stores references and cryptographic identifiers, not secret values.

## 5. Trusted time and event ordering

Wall-clock timestamps supplied by a client are descriptive, not authoritative. Any future time-dependent decision must bind:
- trusted time assertion and source identity;
- timestamp, precision, uncertainty/skew bound and monotonic/order token;
- event ID, issuer and signature/integrity evidence;
- clock synchronization/health evidence;
- rollback, leap adjustment, source outage and conflicting-time behavior.

The authoritative event registry must provide a monotonic sequence/revision or equivalent ordering guarantee. A timestamp alone cannot order concurrent events.

If trusted time is unavailable, inconsistent, outside an approved uncertainty bound, rolled back or unverifiable, fail closed for approval expiry, authorization expiry, evidence freshness and revocation ordering. No component may substitute its local clock or cached timestamp.

No time provider, synchronization protocol, maximum skew, freshness window, grace period or authorization lifetime is selected here. Numeric values remain OPEN until measured, risk-reviewed and owner-approved.

## 6. Approval package and invalidation

An approval binds exact manifest digest, operation, dataset/object versions, policy version, hold/dependency revisions, copy/recovery evidence, requester/reviewer/approver identities, trusted ordering evidence and verification plan.

Any material change to identity, delegation, scope, policy, evidence, hold, dependency, adapter, time trust or verification method invalidates the approval. Reapproval must use a new immutable decision record linked to the prior one.

Approval is not execution authorization. Execution authorization requires a final independent recheck and a separate exact-scope capability.

## 7. Failure and recovery rules

Unknown identity, stale delegation, issuer outage, revocation uncertainty, clock rollback, time-source disagreement, replay, signature failure, audit discontinuity or role collision results in BLOCKED/CONFLICTED. No manual override is permitted unless a separately approved exceptional authority contract defines exact controls; none is approved.

No retry may reuse an invalidated approval or authorization. The new attempt requires fresh identity, policy, hold, dependency, time and evidence checks.
