## 3. Executive result

**No direct contradiction was found** between P1.8.105–P1.8.108 and the governing lifecycle/consumer/copy contracts or owner-approved P1.8.34 directions.

The four documents materially improve the design:

- P1.8.105 prevents a restored snapshot from reviving pre-restore deletion authorization and rejects snapshot-only mutation conclusions.

- P1.8.107 separates time semantics and prohibits inferred ordering across domains.

However, the contracts do not yet establish an integrated, implementation-ready control system. Five findings remain, including three MAJOR integration/authority proof gaps and two MODERATE specification gaps. All are documentary findings; no runtime failure is asserted.

## 5. Findings

### IR109-01 — Restore epoch model is named but not yet sufficiently bound
**Severity: MAJOR — PARTIAL**

P1.8.105 correctly distinguishes Restore Run ID, Restore Epoch, Governance Epoch, snapshot identity and operation-history high-water mark. However, §3.2 and R1 use the combined expression “restore/governance epoch” without freezing:

- whether restore epoch and governance epoch are always separately issued values;
- the authoritative ordering/relationship between them;
- how the epoch is atomically bound to the operation-history high-water mark and governance revisions;
- how concurrent restore attempts or a second restore during reconciliation are serialized;
- how the new epoch becomes the fence observed by every lifecycle decision and mutation authority.

This is not evidence of a current implementation flaw; no implementation exists in this review scope. It is a contract-level ambiguity at the central recovery boundary.

**Required closure:** a versioned epoch-binding crosswalk must define separate identities, issuer/authority, uniqueness scope, ordering, concurrency/fencing, high-water-mark binding, and the exact state that remains quarantined when any binding cannot be proven. Do not choose a provider or numeric epoch format without evidence and separate approval.

### IR109-02 — V08 commit/seal boundary lacks an explicit equality disposition
**Severity: MODERATE — PARTIAL**

P1.8.106 correctly requires one deterministic seal/commit linearization point and classifies unprovable ordering as unresolved. P1.8.34 approves the hybrid event-time/watermark/durable-commit direction and keeps precision/clock values OPEN.

The contract does not explicitly define the disposition when a transaction commit and seal barrier share the same representable timestamp, sequence, boundary token or precision interval. It also does not explicitly require the implementation-specific ordering token/transaction identity to disambiguate that equality. A timestamp comparison alone cannot establish transaction order.

**Required closure:** specify a total-order/linearization token or an explicit rule that every equality/overlap case is UNKNOWN/HOLD until independently ordered. The fixture catalogue must cover before, after, exact equality, overlapping uncertainty, and missing order proof. No wall-clock tie-break or guessed precision is allowed.

### IR109-03 — Trusted-time evidence needs a normative mutation-vector crosswalk
**Severity: MAJOR — PARTIAL**

P1.8.93 requires “trusted ordering/validity evidence” in the protected decision vector and an indivisible mutation-boundary check. P1.8.107 usefully specifies evidence ID, event binding, source/profile, uncertainty, freshness and revocation/authorization context.

However, P1.8.107 does not provide a field-by-field, versioned mapping to P1.8.93's protected vector, nor freeze which evidence revisions and validation results must be rechecked atomically at the mutation boundary. The generic field in P1.8.93 could otherwise be satisfied by a reference that does not pin the complete trusted-time and revocation state required by P1.8.107.

**Required closure:** publish a crosswalk that binds the exact trusted-time evidence object, event/subject, policy/profile revision, trust/revocation state, uncertainty/freshness disposition, authorization expiry/revocation revision and final-check identity into the protected vector. Any material change invalidates the vector. The atomic implementation remains unproven and must stay BLOCKED until a provider/adapter dossier demonstrates the required semantics.

### IR109-04 — TSA evidence dependency and hold mapping is not fully explicit
**Severity: MODERATE — PARTIAL**

P1.8.108 correctly treats imprint inputs, tokens, trust material, revocation evidence, verification profiles/results and manifests as distinct objects. The copy lifecycle contract requires protective hold inheritance by default and blocks disposition where lineage or applicability is incomplete.

P1.8.108 does not yet specify a normative mapping from each evidence object to its parent/child references, consumer/recovery/audit dependencies and inherited hold scope. Without that crosswalk, a future registry could preserve the token while losing a required trust-policy/revocation object, or could incorrectly treat a child evidence object as independently disposable.

**Required closure:** define an evidence-object dependency graph and copy/hold crosswalk: exact object/version, parent/child relationship, required-for-claim edges, applicable holds, copy identities and independent disposition gates. Missing graph edges or uncertain hold applicability must remain protective. Do not introduce automatic cascade deletion.

### IR109-05 — Readiness, quarantine resolution and lifecycle approval roles remain OPEN
**Severity: MAJOR — BLOCKED**

P1.8.105 permits an authorized readiness authority to decide whether a restored scope may leave quarantine. P1.8.107 and P1.8.108 also rely on authorized resolution/review. The deletion contract requires separation among requester, reviewer, approver, authorization authority and executor; P1.8.103 explicitly left the independent-approver or single-owner compensating-control model OPEN.

The new contracts correctly leave the role model OPEN, but they do not yet establish a non-overlapping authority map for restore readiness, V07 quarantine release, TSA evidence verification, lifecycle eligibility, approval, execution authorization and storage execution. Restore READY or quarantine release must never be interpreted as lifecycle approval.

**Required closure:** owner decision and role/authority matrix, including any approved single-owner compensating controls, independent review evidence, conflict handling and scope-limited authority. Until decided, no readiness/quarantine transition or destructive progression can be claimed as authorized.

## 6. Positive invariants confirmed

The review confirms the following documentary properties remain intact:

1. T0 remains the canonical independent data asset. A checkpoint, restore, consumer, TSA token or evidence copy does not acquire authority to mutate/delete T0.
2. CORE_LEDGER and AI_LEDGER remain separate; no new contract merges their state or equity/execution domains.
3. No default raw-data TTL, retention floor, grace period or automatic consumer release was introduced.
4. Consumer ACK is not dependency release; release is limited to the consumer's own exact dependency.
5. Checkpoint seal, trusted timestamp validity, restore completion and historical verification are not deletion eligibility, approval or execution authorization.
6. Source, replica, backup, export, checkpoint, correction and TSA evidence copies retain independent lifecycle identities; no cascade deletion is authorized.
7. Pre-restore execution authorizations are invalidated; snapshot-only object presence/absence cannot establish mutation outcome.
8. Missing, stale, conflicting, ambiguous or unavailable evidence remains protective/fail-closed.
9. No provider, numeric threshold, trust profile or operational capability was inferred.
10. Paper-only boundary remains unchanged: `AI_EXECUTION_ENABLED=false`.
