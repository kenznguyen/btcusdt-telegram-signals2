## 3. Executive assessment

No direct contradiction was found between the approved self-deletion safety invariants and the approved V07/V08/Restore policy directions.

However, the contracts are not yet integrated sufficiently to support implementation or execution:

- V07 has a sound fail-closed direction and a stronger exact-signature timestamp-binding contract, but trust profile, revocation authority, freshness, propagation, clock uncertainty, equality and role parameters remain OPEN.
- V08's immutable checkpoint and append-only correction model is compatible with immutable dataset/version deletion scope, but the deletion eligibility contract does not yet explicitly make watermark closure, seal/commit reconciliation and correction lineage mandatory evidence for every relevant dataset.
- Restore has the strongest operational interaction with deletion: a restored snapshot can reintroduce stale dependencies/holds or stale lifecycle state, while losing newer revocations, tombstones or audit events. Existing fencing and restore requirements point in the right direction, but a single explicit post-restore lifecycle reconciliation/epoch contract is not yet demonstrated.
- TSA evidence is compatible with append-only audit and evidence-copy protection. A timestamp token proves only the bounded existence claim in P1.8.36; it is not deletion authorization, proof of exact signature-creation time, or a substitute for current revocation/hold/dependency checks.

**Overall: PARTIAL.** This means policy compatibility is provisionally acceptable at documentary level, not that the system is ready. Runtime readiness remains NOT VERIFIED; V07/V08/Restore remain blocked on their own gates.

## 5. Required invariants to preserve

The following are binding conclusions for any later design work:

1. **No delete-after-read.** A read, ACK, successful job, checkpoint seal, timestamp validation or restore completion never independently triggers deletion.
2. **No authority transfer.** V07 quarantine, V08 seal, TSA token validity and Restore success do not grant deletion eligibility, approval or execution authority.
3. **No state-domain collapse.** Dataset lifecycle, consumer dependency, hold/quarantine, copy lifecycle, deletion request, authorization, per-object outcome, checkpoint and restore epoch remain distinct domains.
4. **No stale authorization after restore.** Any pre-restore execution authorization is invalid until a new final check and new exact-scope authorization are completed.
5. **No loss of correction lineage.** A sealed checkpoint is immutable; corrections are separately versioned and append-only. Required lineage and replay inputs remain protected.
6. **No evidence co-deletion.** TSA tokens, trust/revocation evidence, manifests, operation history, audit records and tombstones have independently governed lifecycles.
7. **No inferred ordering.** Wall-clock, receipt time, queue order, snapshot age or process completion cannot substitute for the relevant trusted-time or durable linearization proof.
8. **Fail closed.** Missing, stale, ambiguous, contradictory or unavailable evidence blocks the affected decision. In-flight/uncertain mutation follows reconciliation, not a pre-mutation BLOCKED shortcut.
9. **Per-copy governance.** Source data, replicas, backups, exports, corrections and evidence copies are not deleted by cascade.
10. **T0 and ledgers remain protected.** T0 canonical data and CORE_LEDGER/AI_LEDGER protections are unchanged.

## 6. Findings and priority

### CCX-01 — Restore governance epoch and stale authorization invalidation
**Severity: MAJOR — BLOCKED**

The restore contract needs an explicit lifecycle-governance reconciliation boundary. A successful database restore does not establish that the restored dependency/hold/policy/revocation state is current. Pre-restore deletion authorizations must never become usable again after rollback or snapshot restoration.

### CCX-02 — V08 closure evidence is absent from deletion eligibility checklist
**Severity: MAJOR — PARTIAL**

The deletion contract checks recovery and dependencies generically, but does not explicitly require a frozen V08 source-progress/watermark/seal/correction closure record when the target dataset participates in checkpointing. A quiet consumer or elapsed retention period must not mask unsealed or late-arriving data.

### CCX-03 — Shared trusted-time semantics not bound end-to-end
**Severity: MAJOR — PARTIAL**

V07/TSA defines the historical timestamp proof direction, while deletion/fencing requires trusted ordering and freshness. The exact cross-domain authority, uncertainty, freshness and expiry semantics remain OPEN. No clock/provider or numeric value may be inferred.

### CCX-04 — TSA verification evidence lifecycle not explicitly cross-linked
**Severity: MODERATE — PARTIAL**

The evidence-copy contract protects required evidence in principle, but no explicit mapping identifies timestamp tokens, trust-anchor history and revocation evidence as linked evidence objects for deletion/restore decisions.

### CCX-05 — Restore operation history vs object-state oracle
**Severity: MAJOR — BLOCKED**

A restored snapshot cannot independently prove whether an earlier deletion mutation committed. The operation history and object oracle need independent reconciliation, exact object-generation binding and complete manifest coverage.

No direct contradiction or unsafe authority escalation was identified. The MAJOR findings are missing cross-contract proof/ordering requirements, not authorization to implement a workaround.
