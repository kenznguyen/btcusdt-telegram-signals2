## 2. IR145-01 — Bounded source manifest and completeness proof

### 2.1 Assessment boundary

Every future retention-floor assessment must declare a bounded assessment boundary before collecting or interpreting obligation evidence. The boundary must identify:
- exact data class, object ID, version/generation and copy ID(s);
- canonical source identity and known source-to-copy lineage;
- custodians, systems and jurisdictions in scope, where known;
- time interval and policy/contract versions applicable to the object;
- explicit exclusions and the reason each exclusion is safe;
- the intended claim: object-local, copy-local, backup-chain, restore-set or another explicitly named scope.

A scope declaration is not proof that the scope is complete.

### 2.2 Source-of-truth manifest

The assessment record must include a versioned source manifest. For each source expected to reveal an obligation, dependency, hold, copy, custodian or recovery role, record:
- stable source ID, source category and authoritative/non-authoritative designation;
- accountable owner/custodian and the authority for relying on the source;
- covered object classes, systems, locations, time range and known exclusions;
- extraction/snapshot identifier and observation time with timezone/precision;
- freshness basis, integrity/provenance evidence and retrieval outcome;
- reconciliation relationship to other sources and any known overlap;
- accessibility state and whether the source can enumerate all relevant records.

The manifest must consider, where applicable to the declared scope: approved policy and legal/contract sources; consumer and dependency registries; hold and incident sources; source/copy/replica/export catalogs; backup manifests and recovery catalogs; model/training/evaluation and research lineage; audit/security records; external custodians and recipient records; and governance/evidence registries.

This is a required category checklist, not a claim that these systems have been discovered, accessed or inventoried.

### 2.3 Completeness and reconciliation

A completeness claim requires a documented reconciliation showing:
1. every expected source category is either represented by an authoritative source or marked NOT APPLICABLE with a scope-specific rationale;
2. every source in the manifest has a known authority, coverage boundary, observation time and retrieval outcome;
3. overlaps, duplicates, conflicting records and source-to-source count or identity differences are reconciled or explicitly unresolved;
4. source coverage collectively spans the declared object/copy/lineage boundary and relevant obligation interval;
5. known external custodians, recipients and non-enumerable locations are accounted for or explicitly block the claim;
6. stale, inaccessible, partial, non-authoritative or unbounded sources are not treated as complete;
7. the reviewer can trace each obligation row to its originating evidence and each expected source category to a disposition.

Absence of a record in a source that is incomplete, stale, inaccessible, non-authoritative or unable to enumerate its scope is not evidence that the obligation or copy does not exist.

### 2.4 Fail-closed outcomes

If a required source is inaccessible, coverage cannot be established, source authority is disputed, snapshots are stale under an unapproved freshness rule, reconciliation has unexplained differences, or an external custodian/copy cannot be bounded, record the specific limitation and return BLOCKED_UNKNOWN / FLOOR_NOT_ESTABLISHED. Do not estimate around the gap.

No freshness duration or numeric completeness threshold is introduced here. Until approved, adequacy must be justified for the exact claim; uncertainty blocks the claim.

## 3. IR145-02 — Typed obligation states and terminal proof

Each obligation must have one and only one current state from this vocabulary:

| State | Meaning | Floor effect |
|---|---|---|
| ACTIVE | Obligation is in force or its end condition has not been proven | Blocks finite floor |
| SATISFIED | A defined obligation ended through its authoritative satisfaction event | May contribute a proven end only with terminal evidence |
| RELEASED | An authorized authority explicitly released the obligation/hold | May contribute a proven end only with release authority and exact scope |
| SUPERSEDED_WITH_SUCCESSOR | This obligation was replaced by a named successor obligation | Successor must be linked and assessed; predecessor alone cannot close the chain |
| INDEFINITE | Obligation is ongoing or has no defined terminal event | Blocks finite floor |
| UNKNOWN | State, authority, scope or terminal event cannot be established | Blocks finite floor |

### 3.1 Required obligation record

For every obligation, record:
- stable obligation ID, type/domain and exact affected object/version/copy/graph scope;
- issuing/authoritative party, source reference and applicable policy version;
- start condition and time domain;
- current typed state and state-transition history;
- defined terminal condition, if one exists;
- terminal event ID, exact scope, event time and trusted/durable evidence as applicable;
- authority and authorization evidence for a RELEASED state;
- successor obligation ID and continuity evidence for SUPERSEDED_WITH_SUCCESSOR;
- recurrence/renewal schedule or trigger, open-cycle status and next expected event where applicable;
- independent evidence reference and reviewer disposition.

### 3.2 Terminal-state requirements

SATISFIED requires evidence that the obligation's defined completion condition actually occurred for the exact scope. A milestone, elapsed duration, completed processing job or one closed cycle is insufficient if the obligation renews or has an outstanding cycle.

RELEASED requires explicit release by the competent authority, bound to the exact obligation and affected scope. Silence, inactivity, consumer ACK, elapsed time or an operational actor's assertion is not release.

SUPERSEDED_WITH_SUCCESSOR requires a traceable successor ID, effective boundary, scope continuity and evidence that the successor has been included in the assessment. Missing or ambiguous successor linkage is UNKNOWN.

Recurring, rolling, renewable or event-triggered obligations remain ACTIVE while any current cycle is open or a renewal trigger remains applicable. They cannot be converted to a finite end by selecting the last observed cycle.

INDEFINITE has no artificial end. It blocks a finite retention floor unless a competent authority later defines and evidences a valid terminal condition under a separately approved policy.

UNKNOWN always blocks floor establishment.

### 3.3 Aggregation rule

A candidate finite floor may be calculated only if:
- the source-manifest completeness proof in §2 passes for the declared scope;
- every applicable obligation is in a terminal state with valid exact-scope evidence, or a documented NOT APPLICABLE determination is supported by competent authority;
- no ACTIVE, INDEFINITE or UNKNOWN obligation, open recurring cycle, unresolved successor, hold or dependency remains;
- all relevant copy/backup/recovery graph obligations in §4 have been included;
- time-domain binding, event ordering and uncertainty satisfy the controlling trusted-time contracts.

The candidate floor is the latest proven terminal boundary among applicable obligations, interpreted under their approved time semantics. If any condition is unmet, return FLOOR_NOT_ESTABLISHED / RETAIN / BLOCKED. This aggregation is an evidence calculation only and never grants disposition or mutation authority.

## 4. IR145-03 — Copy, backup-chain and restore-set graph semantics

### 4.1 Graph model

Represent each independently managed source, copy, replica, export, backup object, snapshot, manifest, incremental segment, deduplicated/shared storage object and restore point as a distinct lifecycle node with its own identity and lifecycle state.

Represent relationships explicitly, including:
- derived-from / copied-from;
- contains / references;
- requires-for-restore;
- shares-storage-with / deduplicates-to;
- member-of-backup-chain;
- member-of-restore-set;
- exported-to / held-by-custodian;
- supersedes / corrected-by.

Every edge must have provenance, scope, validity interval where applicable and evidence reference. Do not infer edges from matching filenames, timestamps, apparent content equality or physical co-location alone.

### 4.2 Object-local and graph-level obligations

Classify each obligation as:
- OBJECT_LOCAL: applies only to one exact node;
- EDGE/RELATIONSHIP: applies to a specific lineage, custody or dependency edge;
- CHAIN_LEVEL: applies to a defined backup chain or incremental dependency set;
- RESTORE_SET_LEVEL: applies to the complete set required to restore a named recovery point;
- SHARED_RESOURCE: applies to a shared chunk, manifest, index or storage resource used by multiple nodes;
- SCOPE_WIDE: applies to the explicitly declared assessment boundary.

A chain-level or restore-set obligation must identify the complete member set and the exact recovery capability it protects. Shared-resource obligations must identify all known dependent nodes and any unbounded consumers.

### 4.3 Closure and affected-scope calculation

A future assessment must include every graph node and edge needed to establish source lineage, copy completeness, dependency closure and recoverability for the declared claim. It must reconcile graph membership against authoritative manifests and record missing, inaccessible or conflicting members.

An unresolved obligation on a shared node or chain blocks the exact affected scope that depends on it. If the affected set cannot be bounded, the assessment is BLOCKED_UNKNOWN for the broader potentially affected scope.

A graph-level block does not merge lifecycle identities, transfer ownership or authorize cascade disposition. Each node retains its own obligations, custodian, holds, evidence and lifecycle state. No node becomes eligible merely because another copy exists, a source is retained, a chain is old, or a rebuild is theoretically possible.

No source, replica, backup, snapshot, segment, shared chunk, manifest, restore point or export may be removed or altered under this contract.

## 5. IR145-04 — RET-02 noncanonical-copy boundary

RET-02 copy assessment is limited to individually identified **noncanonical** copies within the owner-ratified eligible classes and only as a future read-only exact-object/version assessment.

The following remain protected under RET-01 B:
- T0 canonical data;
- T0 replicas, backups, snapshots, exports or other copies whose role is to preserve T0 canonical availability, integrity, lineage or recovery;
- any copy whose canonical/noncanonical status or operational recovery role is unknown.

No T0 copy becomes reviewable because it is redundant, aged, reproducible, stored outside the primary database, or labelled cache/replica by an implementation.

A noncanonical copy must have its own stable identity and proven role, lineage, custodian, location, obligations, holds, dependencies and recovery status. If its role is ambiguous or it may be required to restore/protect T0 canonical data, classify it PROTECTED / BLOCKED_UNKNOWN.

Any exception to T0 protection requires a separate exact-scope proposal, competent review, independent evidence and a new explicit owner decision. P1.8.146 grants no such exception.

## 6. IR145-05 — Independent reviewer competence and conflict evidence

Every future assessment must identify the reviewer and record:
- reviewer identity and organizational/functional role;
- review scope and the specific claims reviewed;
- competency basis relevant to each claim (for example, data lineage, backup/recovery, security, legal/contract interpretation or model lifecycle);
- relevant training, qualification, experience or delegated authority evidence, without exposing unnecessary personal data;
- explicit conflict-of-interest declaration;
- whether the reviewer had any requester, custodian, obligation-owner, approver, executor or provider-selection role in the assessed matter;
- independence rationale and any compensating control if full role separation is not feasible;
- review date, evidence version, findings and disposition.

The reviewer must be independent of the assessment requester and any future executor for the claims being accepted. A person who created or controls the underlying evidence cannot be the sole independent verifier of that evidence.

Legal, regulatory, contractual, security-authority and specialist determinations must be attributed to a competent authority or specialist for the relevant jurisdiction/domain. A technical reviewer may verify that such evidence exists and is correctly scoped; they must not invent or substitute the substantive legal/security conclusion.

If reviewer competence, independence, conflict status or specialist attribution is missing or disputed, record INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED / BLOCKED. A review result does not approve deletion, archive or mutation.

## 8. Required state and decision boundaries

This supplement introduces no new disposition state and does not alter P1.8.144's permitted documentary outcomes.

- Incomplete source coverage, unknown obligation state, unbounded recurring obligation, missing successor, incomplete copy graph, ambiguous canonical role or inadequate reviewer independence => BLOCKED_UNKNOWN / RETAIN / FLOOR_NOT_ESTABLISHED.
- A fully evidenced floor may at most become FLOOR_ESTABLISHED_FOR_REVIEW.
- FLOOR_ESTABLISHED_FOR_REVIEW is not deletion eligibility, deletion approval, execution authorization or proof of technical enforcement.
- Option C — No Destructive Authority — remains in force. Archive and irreversible transitions remain non-executable.
