# P5.4.82C — Custody Jurisdiction and Account Ownership Decision Proposal

**Date:** 2026-11-04  
**Status:** DEFERRED — LEGAL/OWNERSHIP FACTS REQUIRED; MODEL A/B/C NOT SELECTED  
**Scope:** D82-C only; jurisdiction, legal ownership, provider-account ownership and administrative separation for primary KMS/HSM and independent escrow recovery authority.

## 1. Purpose

D82-A selected an independent escrow authority for recovery and the primary KMS/HSM for routine operations. D82-B approved a role-based 2-of-3 recovery quorum. Neither decision selected a jurisdiction, legal entity, account owner, provider, custodian or cloud account.

This proposal defines the decisions needed before comparing or provisioning providers. It does not provide legal advice and does not infer the user's country of residence, corporate status, tax status, data-residency obligations or eligible contracting entity.

## 2. Constraints already approved

- Primary KMS/HSM is for routine cryptographic operations; independent escrow is the recovery authority.
- D82-B 2-of-3 approval remains mandatory; requester and executor are excluded; alternates must be designated before an incident; no emergency bypass.
- D6 restore epoch, old-writer stop proof, lease re-fencing, staging verification, publication, Recovery Latch, Kill Switch and writer-admission gates remain separate.
- No single person or shared administrator credential may collapse the independent trust domains.
- No provider, jurisdiction, legal entity, account or custodian is currently selected.
- No provisioning, key generation, identity enrollment or production access is authorized.

## 3. Decision dimensions

D82-C must keep these concepts distinct; they are not interchangeable:

1. **Governing jurisdiction:** applicable law and regulatory jurisdiction for each contracting entity and service.
2. **Data location/residency:** where encrypted backup payloads, metadata, audit evidence and replicas are stored or processed.
3. **Legal ownership:** the entity or person that legally owns/controls the backup data, encryption keys and recovery rights.
4. **Provider account ownership:** contracting account owner, billing owner and organization-level administrator.
5. **Operational administration:** identities allowed to configure policy, grant access, rotate/revoke keys or administer storage.
6. **Escrow independence:** whether the escrow custodian and its administrative control plane are independent from routine KMS/HSM operations and the primary account administrators.

## 4. Options

### A — One legal owner and one provider organization, separated accounts/projects

**Advantages**
- Lowest administrative and contractual complexity.
- Centralized billing, governance and incident response.
- Separate accounts/projects can isolate IAM, keys, logs and storage policies.

**Risks**
- Organization-level administrators, billing recovery or identity-provider compromise may create a common control plane.
- Escrow may not be sufficiently independent if controlled by the same owner/admin group.
- Common legal, provider and jurisdictional failure domains remain.

**Suitable only if**
- Technical separation is enforceable and tested.
- Independent escrow remains outside the same privileged administrator boundary.
- A documented threat model accepts common legal/provider dependencies.

### B — One legal owner; primary KMS/HSM and escrow in separate provider organizations/accounts

**Advantages**
- Stronger technical and administrative separation without requiring multiple legal entities.
- Clearer separation between routine key operations and recovery authority.
- Can reduce common-mode compromise of a single provider organization.

**Risks**
- Cross-provider integration and identity federation complexity.
- Still shares the same legal owner and may share jurisdictional, corporate and identity dependencies.
- Separate provider organizations do not automatically prove independent people or governance.

**Suitable only if**
- Independent account administrators and authentication roots are demonstrated.
- Escrow contractually and operationally controls its recovery function.
- Cross-provider export, residency and exit paths are verified.

### C — Separate legal entities and separate provider organizations/accounts

**Advantages**
- Strongest potential separation of legal ownership, contracting and privileged administration.
- Better ability to establish independent escrow governance and failure domains.

**Risks**
- Highest legal, tax, compliance, contracting, audit and operational overhead.
- Requires valid entity structure, authority, contracts, data-processing terms and succession arrangements.
- Could be impractical or create new recovery bottlenecks if entities are not genuinely independent.

**Suitable only if**
- Qualified legal/compliance review confirms the structure and responsibilities.
- Ownership, control, data-processing and recovery rights are explicit and enforceable.
- Actual independence is evidenced rather than assumed from entity names.

## 5. Recommended decision posture

**Recommendation: do not select A, B or C yet.** First complete a jurisdiction and ownership fact sheet, then obtain qualified legal/compliance review where applicable. The current evidence does not establish which jurisdictions or legal entities are valid for this project.

For subsequent design, use these minimum separation requirements regardless of the eventual option:
- Escrow recovery authority must not be administered through the same privileged account/administrator group as the primary KMS/HSM.
- Separate privileged identities and authentication roots; no shared break-glass credential.
- Distinct account owners and administrators must be explicitly named and evidenced before provisioning.
- Contractual data ownership, key custody, recovery authority, service termination, export and deletion rights must be documented.
- Data location for ciphertext, manifests, audit logs, replicas and support/processing must be recorded separately.
- Cross-border transfer, export, retention and legal-hold constraints must be reviewed before selecting storage regions.
- Provider exit and recovery must not depend solely on the primary provider or its identity plane.
- No provider may be selected solely because it offers a different account or region.

These are design requirements, not evidence that any provider currently satisfies them.

## 6. Owner decisions required

The owner must explicitly decide:
1. **Ownership model:** A, B, C, or defer selection pending legal/organizational facts.
2. **Legal data owner:** exact person/entity and its authority to control backup data and recovery rights. Do not invent or infer an entity.
3. **Primary KMS/HSM account owner:** accountable contracting owner and organization administrator.
4. **Escrow account/custodian owner:** independent contracting owner and administrator, separate from primary privileged administration.
5. **Jurisdiction and data location:** permitted jurisdictions/regions for each of payload, manifest, audit evidence, replicas and processing. If unknown, mark unresolved; do not assume a country or region.
6. **Independence standard:** whether separation must include distinct legal entities, distinct provider organizations, distinct identity providers, or a combination.
7. **Legal/compliance review gate:** who is qualified and authorized to validate ownership, residency, export, privacy, retention and cross-border obligations.

The owner may approve the minimum separation principles while leaving named entities and regions unresolved. Such approval does not permit provisioning.

## 7. Evidence required before provider selection

- Documented legal owner and proof of authority to contract and control recovery.
- Provider terms and service-specific documentation for data location, subprocessors, support access, backup/replication and deletion.
- Account/organization ownership and privileged administrator boundary diagrams.
- Evidence that primary KMS/HSM and escrow have separate privileged identity and administrative roots.
- Data-flow inventory for encrypted payload, manifest, wrapped DEK metadata, signatures, audit evidence and replicas.
- Residency/export/retention/legal-hold review by a qualified person for the applicable jurisdictions.
- Provider exit, export, key portability and escrow recovery test plan.
- Threat model covering provider/identity-plane compromise, legal owner unavailability, account suspension, insolvency and jurisdictional outage.

No provider comparison is complete without these inputs.

## 8. Fail-closed rules

- Unknown legal owner, contracting authority, jurisdiction or data location => BLOCKED_D82C_UNRESOLVED.
- Unproven separation between primary KMS/HSM and escrow privileged administration => BLOCKED_TRUST_BOUNDARY.
- Unclear ownership or recovery rights => no key generation, provisioning or backup upload.
- Residency/export/retention conflict or uncertainty => no data placement in the affected region.
- Provider/account suspension or loss of one control plane must not trigger fallback to shared credentials or a single administrator.
- A D82-C decision cannot weaken D82-B quorum or any D6 gate.
